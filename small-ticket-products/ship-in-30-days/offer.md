## The offer page

### On day 30, a stranger downloads your app.

Not a prototype. Not a portfolio piece. A real app, in a real store, with your name on it.

Here's the thing about why people do not ship. It is not talent and it is almost never time. It is that there is no process, so every night is a fresh set of decisions, and fresh decisions at 11pm are how projects die quietly in a folder called `projects/old`.

This is thirty days of not having to decide. **One task a day, about an hour.** Day 1 you write a sentence. Day 30 you press release.

**The four weeks:**

- **Week 1, decide and validate.** The week everybody skips and the one that decides whether the other three matter. Includes the highest-value hour of the whole month: reading the one and two star reviews of your competitors and writing down exactly what people hate. Those words become your App Store description on day 22.
- **Week 2, build the core.** Starter kit running, your colors in, the core loop working ugly by day 10. Because if it is not good when it is ugly, no amount of design will save it, and you want to learn that on day 10 rather than day 25.
- **Week 3, make it real.** Empty states, settings, onboarding, real devices, polish, icons. The unglamorous week that separates an app from a demo.
- **Week 4, ship it.** Store copy, screenshots, legal, the sixty-point pre-flight, submit on day 26 with manual release, handle the rejection that probably comes, and go live on day 30.

**What comes with it:**

- The full day-by-day outline, every task with a clear "done when"
- **The Indie App Starter Kit** ($79 on its own), so day 8 starts from a working app rather than an empty folder
- **The Launch Playbook** ($27) and **the ASO Cheat Sheet** ($19)
- Three group calls: kickoff, day 15, and launch week
- A shared channel, questions answered within one business day
- One 20-minute one-to-one, whenever you need it most
- Accountability emails at day 7, 14, 21, 28, and 30, on the days people usually quit

**$199. Cohorts of 8 to 12, quarterly. Your ship date is a real calendar date and you will tell people about it.**

> Two honest things. First, on the kickoff call I am going to make you cut your idea down, and you will not enjoy it. Everyone who fails this challenge fails because their day 1 idea was too big, never because their day 15 effort was too small.
>
> Second, launch day for a first app with no audience is quiet. Dozens of downloads, not thousands. That is normal and it is not failure. But on day 31 you are a person who ships, and that is a different person from the one who started.

## Notes and assumptions

- **Priced at $199.** It includes $125 of existing products from this library at their listed prices, which makes the facilitation feel close to free and gives the price page an easy justification.
- **Cohorts of 8 to 12, quarterly, never back to back.** About 20 hours of Dillon's time per cohort: 3 hours of calls plus 30 to 45 minutes a day in the channel. At 10 people that is roughly $2,000 for 20 hours, which is fine on its own, and the real return is that a meaningful share of participants become build clients or partnership candidates.
- **The facilitation runbook is the actual product here.** It maps where people quit day by day (day 10 and day 29 are the two big ones) and gives Dillon a specific move for each, including posting an ugly screenshot of one of his own apps at day 10, which does more for completion than any amount of encouragement.
- **Ship rate is the metric,** and the runbook says to publish it honestly including the misses. "9 of 12 shipped" is more credible and sells better than an implication that everyone did.
- **The web-based Starter Kit makes this achievable for non-experts,** which widens the audience considerably. A native iOS version of the challenge would need a Mac, an Apple developer account, and Xcode competence on day 8. This version needs a browser and a text editor.
- Run the first cohort at a reduced price with a small group to find where the outline breaks. The runbook predicts day 10 and day 22, and it is worth finding out before charging full price.
- Best upsell path: challenge, then Application Creation or the partnership program, for the participants who finish and immediately want to build something bigger.
