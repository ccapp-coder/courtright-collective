# Review Templates

Swap the brackets. Say it out loud once to make sure it sounds like you. Then save it where you can reach it in five seconds, because a template you have to hunt for is a template you will not use.

---

## 1. In-person asks

**The standard one**

> "I'm really glad that worked out. Would you mind leaving us a Google review? It genuinely helps people find us. I can text you the link right now if that's easier."

**After a compliment**

> "That means a lot, thank you. Honestly, if you'd put that exact sentence in a Google review, it would help us more than you know. Want me to text you the link?"

**Service business, at the end of a job**

> "Before I head out, one small ask. We're a small operation and Google reviews are basically how people find us. Takes about thirty seconds. I'll text you the link now while I'm thinking about it."

**Restaurant or retail, at the register**

> "Hope we'll see you again. If you've got a second sometime, a Google review really helps us out. There's a code right here on the counter."

**Repeat customer**

> "You've been coming here a while now, which honestly makes my week. Would you consider leaving a Google review? Coming from a regular, it carries a lot of weight."

**After they refer somebody**

> "Thanks for sending `[name]` our way, that's the highest compliment there is. If you ever have thirty seconds, a Google review would help us reach more people like you."

**When you are not sure how they felt**

> "How did everything go? ... Good, I'm glad. We ask everybody the same thing, so here it is: if you'd leave a Google review, good or bad, we'd appreciate it. It's how we get better and it's how people find us."

*(Ask everybody the same way. That is not just the honest approach, it is the one that keeps you inside Google's rules.)*

## 2. Text message asks

Keep them under 160 characters. Send within an hour, while you are still fresh in their mind.

**Standard**

> Hey `[name]`, `[your name]` from `[business]`. Great working with you today. If you have 30 seconds, a Google review helps us a ton: `[link]` Thanks either way.

**Service completed**

> `[Name]`, all finished and cleaned up. Thanks for having us out. If you'd leave a quick review it really helps a small shop like ours: `[link]`

**Restaurant**

> Thanks for coming in tonight! If you enjoyed it, a quick Google review makes a big difference for us: `[link]`

**Appointment based**

> Hi `[name]`, thanks for coming in today. Quick favor if you have a minute: `[link]` Appreciate you.

**Follow-up, three days later, send once**

> Hi `[name]`, `[your name]` again. No pressure at all, just floating this back up in case it got buried: `[link]` Either way, thanks for your business.

## 3. Email asks

**Standard**

> Subject: One small favor
>
> Hey `[name]`,
>
> Thanks again for `[the specific thing]`. Genuinely enjoyed working with you.
>
> One small favor if you have a minute. We're a small business and Google reviews are how most people find us. If you'd leave one, it would mean a lot:
>
> `[link]`
>
> Takes about thirty seconds. And if something wasn't right, reply to this email instead and I'll fix it.
>
> Best,
> `[Your name]`

**Short version, which usually beats the long one**

> Subject: 30 seconds?
>
> Hey `[name]`,
>
> Would you leave us a Google review? It helps more than anything else we do.
>
> `[link]`
>
> Thanks either way.
>
> `[Your name]`

**Add it to your existing invoice or receipt email**

> P.S. If we did right by you, a Google review is the best thing you could do for us: `[link]`

**Add it to your email signature**

> `[Your name]` | `[Business]`
> `[Phone]` | `[Website]`
> **Happy with our work? [Leave us a review]** `[link]`

**Follow-up, once, three days later**

> Subject: re: One small favor
>
> Hey `[name]`,
>
> Bumping this up one time in case it got buried. Totally fine to ignore.
>
> `[link]`
>
> Best,
> `[Your name]`

## 5. Replies to positive reviews

Two sentences. Specific to what they said. Include something useful for the next person reading it.

**Five stars, detailed**

> Thank you `[name]`, this made our day. Really glad `[the specific thing they mentioned]` worked out, and we'll pass that along to `[team member if named]`. See you next time.

**Five stars, no text**

> Thanks for the five stars, `[name]`. We appreciate you taking the time.

**Five stars, mentions a specific person**

> `[Name]`, thank you. `[Employee]` is going to be thrilled to read this, and honestly they have earned it. Thanks for taking the time to say so.

**Five stars, repeat customer**

> Always good to see you, `[name]`. Thanks for being a regular and thanks for writing this. It means a lot coming from someone who has been around a while.

**Four stars**

> Thanks for the review, `[name]`. Glad `[the good thing]` worked for you. If there's something that would have made it a five, I'd genuinely like to hear it. `[email]` comes straight to me.

## 6. Replies to negative reviews

**The formula:** thank them, name the specific problem, say what you are doing about it, give a direct contact. Four sentences. No arguing, no policy explanation, no listing what they got wrong.

**One star, valid complaint**

> Thank you for telling us, `[name]`, and I'm sorry. You're right that `[the specific problem]` is not how it should have gone. We've `[the specific change you made]` so it does not happen again. If you'd give me a chance to make it right, I'm at `[email or phone]` and it comes straight to me.

**One star, service failure**

> `[Name]`, this one is on us. `[Acknowledge the specific failure without excuses.]` I've talked with the team about `[the fix]`. Please reach me directly at `[contact]`, I'd like to sort this out with you.

**One star, wait time or delay**

> Sorry about the wait, `[name]`. `[Day or time]` got away from us and that's not the experience we want to give anybody. We've `[the specific staffing or process change]`. If you come back, ask for me and I'll take care of you personally.

**Two or three stars, mixed**

> Thanks for the honest review, `[name]`. Glad `[the good part]` landed, and you're right about `[the bad part]`. We're working on it. If you want to tell me more, `[contact]` reaches me directly.

**One star, unfair or mistaken**

> Thanks for the feedback, `[name]`. I looked into this and it looks like `[the factual clarification, in one calm sentence]`. If I've got that wrong, please reach out at `[contact]` and I'll happily take another look.

*(This reply is not for them. It is for the next hundred people who read it. Calm always wins that audience.)*

**One star, wrong business**

> Hi `[name]`, thanks for writing, but I think this might be meant for another business. We're `[business]` at `[address]` and I can't find a record of this visit. If I'm mistaken, please reach me at `[contact]` and I'll sort it out right away.

**One star, no text at all**

> Sorry to see this, `[name]`. I'd genuinely like to know what went wrong so we can fix it. `[Contact]` comes straight to me.

**One star, clearly a competitor or spam**

> This does not match any customer record we have. We've reported it to Google for review. If you are an actual customer and I've got this wrong, please contact me directly at `[contact]` and I'll make it right.

*(Then report it in your profile, once, and let it go.)*

## 7. The rules, one more time

Every template above works because it follows these:

- **Ask everybody the same way.** Never screen for happy customers first
- **Never offer anything in exchange.** No discount, no free item, no drawing, no points
- **Never write your own reviews,** and neither does your team
- **Ask once, follow up once, stop**
- **Reply to everything,** within 48 hours
