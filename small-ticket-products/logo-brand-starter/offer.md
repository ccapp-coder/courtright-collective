## Sales blurb

**A brand is not a logo. A brand is the set of decisions you stop re-making.**

Right now, every time you post something, print something, or build a page, you are re-deciding what color to use, what font looks right, and how formal to sound. That is exhausting, it is slow, and it is exactly why your stuff does not look like it came from the same place.

Answer fourteen questions. Never decide any of it again.

**The worksheet** walks you through it in an afternoon: what you do in one sentence, who it is for specifically, what genuinely makes you different (the honest answer, not the marketing one), your competitors' visual pattern and whether you are joining it or breaking it on purpose, your name and the six-part name test, your mark, your five colors, your two fonts, and both halves of your voice.

**The brand board builder** turns those answers into a real one-page board, live in your browser:

- Your logo lockup, three mark shapes
- Your full palette, **with every color automatically graded for readability** against your background. Body text, large text only, or not for text at all
- A type specimen showing headings, body, and small print in your actual fonts
- Buttons, badges, and links rendered in your colors so you see how they behave
- Your voice, both what you sound like and what you never sound like
- **Usage rules that write themselves,** including an automatic warning naming any color of yours that fails contrast and must never carry small text

Print it, save it as a PDF, or download it as a self-contained HTML file to hand to anybody who makes anything for you.

**$39. One afternoon. Then stop thinking about it.**

> The contrast grading is the part that actually matters. Unreadable color is the single most common mistake in small business branding, and it is the one that quietly makes people give up on your website without ever telling you why. The builder catches it before you print a thousand cards.

> And the hardest rule in the whole worksheet: do not change it for six months, even when you get bored of it. You will get sick of your brand long before your customers notice it exists. Consistency is what makes a small business look established, and the only way to be consistent is to stop fiddling.

## Notes and assumptions

- Priced at $39. Fiverr logos are $50 and produce a file with no system around it. This produces the system, which is the part that actually keeps a business looking put together.
- **Ambiguity call, noted as instructed.** "Logo + brand starter" could have implied producing actual logo artwork. Generating a real custom logo is a design engagement, not a template, so the product does the honest version: a monogram mark that works at every size, plus the full decision framework around it. The worksheet says out loud that a monogram is a legitimate logo for a small business, and separately says not to use a generic house, leaf, swoosh, gear, or lightbulb icon, because everyone in every category already has.
- **The contrast maths is real WCAG 2 relative luminance and was verified:** black on white returns exactly 21:1 and #777777 on white returns 4.48:1, both matching the published values. The rules list is generated from the buyer's actual color choices, so the warnings are specific rather than generic.
- Font choices are limited to system fonts on purpose. No downloads, no licensing questions, no web font that fails to load and dumps the buyer back to Times New Roman on somebody's work laptop.
- **States its own limits honestly at the end:** this gets you a solid, consistent, professional brand you can run for years. It does not get you a custom illustrated identity from a designer who spent three weeks on your story, and it does not pretend to. That paragraph will sell more copies than it costs.
