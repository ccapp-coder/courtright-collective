## Sales blurb

**You sent that song to somebody. You are almost sure you did.**

Here's the thing about pitching songs. The writing is the part you love. The tracking is the part that actually decides whether anything happens.

You send a song on a Tuesday. Three weeks later you are in a room with somebody and you genuinely cannot remember whether you already sent them that one, or whether they passed on it, or whether it is still on hold somewhere else and you should not be pitching it at all. So you either double-pitch, which is embarrassing, or you say nothing, which is worse.

Multiply that by a catalog and a couple of years. That is real opportunity going in the trash. Not because the songs are not good. Because nobody wrote it down.

**Three sheets, five minute setup:**

- **The Pitch Tracker.** One row per pitch, not per song. Status, hold dates, follow-up dates, follow-up counts, and their exact words pasted in rather than your summary of them. Because "not right for this project but send more" is a completely different message from "pass," and in six months you will not remember which one you got.
- **The Song Catalog.** Every song with splits, demo status, keys, tempo, who it is for, and whether the paperwork is done. Includes the filter that finds every unsigned split sheet in your catalog, which is the single most preventable disaster in songwriting.
- **Contacts.** Who they are, what they cut, what they do not want, what format they prefer, and how warm the relationship is. Sort by warmth before every pitch round.

**Plus the setup guide:** conditional formatting rules so overdue follow-ups turn red on their own, eight copy-and-paste formulas, the follow-up rhythm that works (day 0, day 10, day 30, day 90 and then let it go), and the 20 minute Monday routine that runs the whole thing.

Works in Google Sheets, Excel, Numbers, Notion, or Airtable. No app to buy, no subscription, no account.

**$19. Set it up in five minutes. Run it in twenty minutes a week.**

> Straight with you: a tracker does not get you a cut. Songs get you cuts and relationships get your songs heard. What this does is stop the work you already did from getting quietly wasted because you forgot who had what. If you have been doing this more than a year, you already know that is a real and expensive problem.

## Notes and assumptions

- Priced at $19. Songwriters are not a high-budget audience and this needs to be an obvious yes.
- Built as CSV rather than a Google Sheets share link on purpose: buyers own the file, it works in whatever tool they already use, and there is no permissions mess when someone shares the link around.
- All sample rows use placeholder names (`Sample Contact A`, `Sample Publishing`). No real industry names anywhere, which matters for a Nashville audience where everyone knows everyone.
- The 20 minute Monday routine in the guide is the retention hook. It is also what buyers will quote when they recommend it.
- Best upsell path: Tracker, then Website Creation for their artist site, then the AI automations for follow-up email.
- Good bundle partner: Solopreneur CRM Starter for writers who also do session work or teach. Call it "The Working Musician Kit" at $29.
