## Sales blurb

**Your email signature is currently four lines of blue underlined text and a broken image icon.**

Not your fault. Email is not the web. Email clients run rendering engines from a decade ago, strip out anything they do not like, and block images by default. So the signature that looked great when you built it shows up on the other end as a red X and a wall of Times New Roman.

Seven signatures that do not do that, plus a builder that fills them in for you.

**Open the builder in your browser. Type your details. Pick a style. Click copy.** That is the whole product. Nothing gets uploaded, nothing gets sent, it all runs locally in the file you downloaded.

**The seven styles:**

- **The Classic.** Serif name, copper rule, all the basics
- **The Minimal.** Two lines, for people who reply sixty times a day and do not want a billboard under every one
- **The Monogram.** A colored initials block instead of a logo image. Looks designed, needs zero image hosting, never breaks
- **The Booking CTA.** A real button that survives Outlook, for anyone whose business runs on booked calls
- **The Social.** Text-based social links, because icons are the number one reason signatures look broken
- **The Promo Banner.** A rotating announcement strip. Change the line once a month and you get free advertising on every email you were already sending
- **The Two Column.** For addresses, hours, license numbers, and anyone with a lot to say

**Why these hold together:** tables for layout because Outlook renders email with the Microsoft Word engine and Word does not do flexbox. Inline styles only, because Gmail strips style blocks. Zero images anywhere. Web-safe fonts. Bulletproof table-cell buttons.

**Plus the install guide:** step by step for Gmail, new Outlook, classic Outlook desktop, Apple Mail, and iPhone. Including the Apple Mail checkbox that flattens your formatting if you miss it, and the workaround for when your browser pastes it as plain text anyway.

**$15. Five minutes. Then every email you send looks like you meant it.**

> Also in there: the seven rules for a signature that does its job. Short version, four lines max, one link that matters, no headshot, and no inspirational quote. Especially no inspirational quote.
