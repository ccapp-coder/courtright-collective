## The offer page

### Somebody is on your website right now, deciding whether to leave.

You cannot see them do it. You just see a number in analytics that never moves, and you assume you need more traffic.

Usually you do not need more traffic. You need the people already showing up to stop leaving.

**I will go through your site the way a stranger does.** Cold, on a phone, with no idea who you are. Then I write down exactly what happened, what it cost you, and the five things to fix first, in order.

**What you get:**

- **The eight second test.** What a stranger understands about your business before they decide to stay or go. This one is usually uncomfortable and always useful.
- **Above the fold,** on mobile and desktop, and what it is actually communicating.
- **The path to contacting you,** counted in taps. It should be one. It usually is not, and the reason is usually something small and fixable.
- **Your copy, quoted back to you,** with specific rewrites for your headline, your subhead, your main button, and the first line of your About page. Most people have not read their own website in two years.
- **The trust checklist.** Eight things a stranger looks for before handing over money, and which ones you are missing.
- **Speed and mobile,** the seven checks that actually matter.
- **Getting found,** the SEO basics that are either there or they are not.
- **The five things to fix first,** ranked by impact divided by effort, each with what it costs you to ignore, how long it takes, and how to do it.
- **What I would leave alone.** Because half of website advice is somebody talking you into changing things that were already working.

**$99. Three business days. One PDF, written by a person, about your specific site.**

No automated scan, no 47-page report generated by a tool, no score out of 100 that tells you nothing. Just me, your site, and about an hour.

**And everything in it is yours regardless.** Take it to any developer you like. If you would rather I just did the work, the last page tells you what that costs, once, with no follow-up sequence.

> Fair warning: if the honest answer is that your site is fine and you should spend the money on something else, that is what the teardown will say. I would rather lose one project than sell somebody a rebuild they did not need.

## Notes and assumptions

- **Priced at $99.** High enough to filter out tire-kickers and to be worth an hour of real work, low enough that a small business owner can approve it without a conversation. This is the classic paid-discovery play: the deliverable is genuinely valuable on its own, and it makes the follow-on sale a formality when it is warranted.
- **Realistic time budget is 60 to 75 minutes,** broken down in the internal notes at the bottom of the delivery template. At $99 that is a sane effective rate, and the template is what keeps it from sprawling into three hours.
- **The delivery template does most of the work.** It is structured so the writing is filling in shapes rather than composing from scratch, which is the only way a productized service stays profitable.
- **Section 9, "What I would leave alone," is the conversion engine.** It proves the teardown is not just a list of reasons to hire you, which is exactly what makes people hire you.
- The internal notes block at the end of the template must be deleted before sending. It is marked clearly.
- Cap these at a handful a month. It is a lead generator, not a business line, and the moment it becomes a volume product the quality that makes it work disappears.
- Best upsell path: Teardown, then the five fixes as a fixed-price job, then Website Creation or a maintenance retainer.
