## Sales blurb

**You handed your kid a tablet this week and then felt weird about it.**

Been there. Am there. I have two kids, I build toddler apps for a living, and I still had a moment last month in a restaurant where I passed over my phone and thought "well, I am the screen time guy, this is going great."

So let's take the temperature down.

Here's the thing that took me way too long to figure out. Screen time is not one thing. Twenty minutes of a kid tapping shapes and naming colors while you are in the room is not the same activity as ninety minutes of autoplay with a hard cut every four seconds. Same clock. Completely different experience for their brain.

Almost every guilt spiral about screens comes from measuring the wrong thing. We count minutes because minutes are easy to count.

**Inside this guide:**

- **What the guidance actually says,** including the part that got left out of the headline everybody remembers
- **The three questions that replace counting minutes.** Are they doing something or having something done to them? Am I anywhere nearby? And how is the landing when it ends? That last one tells you more than any timer will
- **How to spot a good toddler app in ten seconds.** Eight green flags, seven red flags, and the ten second test that sorts them
- **Slots instead of stopwatches,** and why "after lunch" ends the constant asking in a way that "45 minutes" never will
- **How to give the ending a shape,** so turning it off stops being a fight. Four things that work, including handing them the power button
- **The one thing to be genuinely strict about** (it is the hour before bed, and it is not close)
- **What screens are actually good at,** written out plainly, because that part always gets left out
- **The guilt part.** Short version: the guilt is doing more damage to your house than the twenty minutes of tapping
- **A one-page fridge version** you can print and stop thinking about

No lecturing. No fear. Nobody is telling you to unplug your child and move to a cabin.

**$9. About a 20 minute read. Then you can stop apologizing to yourself about it.**

> Written by a dad who reads too much about this, not by a doctor. Your pediatrician knows your kid and I do not. The guide says so, out loud, twice.
