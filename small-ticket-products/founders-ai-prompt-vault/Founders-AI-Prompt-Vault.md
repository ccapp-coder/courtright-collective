# Founder's AI Prompt Vault

## Start here

Most people use AI like a search engine. They type six words, get something generic, decide AI is overrated, and go back to doing it by hand. I did the same thing for about a month.

Here's the thing. The model is not the problem. The prompt is the problem. A good prompt does four jobs: it gives the AI a role, it hands over the real context, it says exactly what shape the answer should take, and it sets the bar for what "good" means.

Every prompt in this vault does all four. Paste it in, swap the brackets for your real details, and you get something you can actually use instead of something you have to rewrite.

**Works with:** Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, whatever you already pay for. These are model-agnostic on purpose.

**How to import to Notion:** Notion, then `Import`, then `Markdown`, drop this file in. Every category becomes its own section, and each prompt block copies cleanly.

---

## The four rules that make any prompt better

1. **Give it a job title.** "You are a bookkeeper who has cleaned up 300 messy small business ledgers" beats "help me with my books" every time.
2. **Feed it your actual mess.** Paste the real email, the real numbers, the real half-finished draft. AI is unbelievably good at editing and unbelievably average at inventing.
3. **Name the format.** Table, five bullets, 80 words, subject line plus body. If you do not say, you get an essay.
4. **Tell it what bad looks like.** "No corporate filler, no em dashes, do not use the word leverage." Constraints do more work than instructions.

And one bonus rule that changes everything: **ask it to ask you questions first.** Add "Before you answer, ask me up to five questions you need answered to do this well" to any prompt in this vault. The output gets dramatically better.

## 1. Marketing and copy

### 1.1 The homepage headline machine

```
You are a direct response copywriter who writes for small businesses,
not tech startups. Plain language, no jargon, no em dashes.

My business: [what you do, one sentence]
My customer: [who they are, and what they were googling right before
they found me]
The result I deliver: [the specific outcome, with a number if you have one]
What makes me different: [the honest answer, not the marketing answer]

Write 10 homepage headlines. For each one, add a single-sentence
subhead. Vary the angle: outcome, contrarian, question, direct offer.
Then tell me which one you would bet on and why, in two sentences.
```

### 1.2 Rewrite my About page so it sounds human

```
Here is my current About page: [paste it]

Rewrite it so it sounds like a real person wrote it. Keep every fact.
Cut the puffery. Target 200 words. Use short sentences mixed with
longer ones for rhythm. No em dashes, no "passionate about," no
"we're a team of dedicated professionals."

Give me two versions: one warm and personal, one confident and direct.
```

### 1.3 Turn one idea into a week of posts

```
Core idea: [the one thing you want to say]
Audience: [who]
Platform: [LinkedIn / Instagram / Facebook]
My voice: [three adjectives, plus one thing you never say]

Give me 7 posts built off that one idea. Vary the format across the
week: a teaching list, a behind the scenes moment, a customer win,
a question that invites replies, a myth I want to kill, a short
opinion, and a soft promo.

Each post under 120 words. Include a first line that earns the second
line. No hashtag walls, three max.
```

### 1.4 The ad angle generator

```
Product: [what it is]
Price: [price]
Buyer: [who]
Top objection: [the real reason people hesitate]

Give me 8 different ad angles. Each angle needs: the hook line, the
core promise, and the single objection it defeats. Do not repeat the
same emotional lever twice. Then rank them by how fast a cold
stranger would understand the offer.
```

### 1.5 Steal from my best customer's own words

```
Here are reviews and messages from my happiest customers: [paste them]

1. Pull out the exact phrases they use to describe the problem and
   the result. Verbatim, not paraphrased.
2. Group them into themes.
3. Tell me which three phrases I should be using on my website that
   I am probably not.
4. Write three headlines using only their words.
```

### 1.6 Landing page audit

```
Here is my landing page copy: [paste it]
The one action I want: [the CTA]

Review it as a skeptical first-time visitor with 8 seconds of
attention. Answer:
- What do you think I sell, after reading only the first two lines?
- What is confusing?
- What is missing that would make you trust me?
- Where does it get boring?
Then give me a rewritten version of the top third only.
```

### 1.7 The email subject line bank

```
Email topic: [what the email is about]
Audience: [who is on the list, and how they got there]
Goal: [open, click, reply, buy]

Give me 15 subject lines. Five curiosity, five direct, five that
read like a personal note from a friend. Max 45 characters each.
Flag any that would trip a spam filter and say why.
```

## 2. Sales and follow-up

### 2.1 The follow-up nobody hates

```
Context: I spoke with [name] at [company] on [date] about [topic].
What they said: [their exact words or your notes]
What I want next: [the next step]

Write a follow-up email under 120 words. Reference something
specific they said so it is obvious I listened. End with one
question that is easy to answer with a single line. No pressure
language, no "just circling back," no em dashes.
```

### 2.2 Turn a discovery call into a proposal

```
Here are my notes from the call: [paste the messy notes]

Turn these into a proposal with:
- One paragraph restating their problem in their own words
- The outcome we are aiming for, stated as something measurable
- Scope: exactly what I will deliver, as a bulleted list
- Timeline with milestones
- Price: [price], stated once, no apologizing
- What I need from them
- What is explicitly not included

Tone: confident and plain. This should read like a person, not a
law firm.
```

### 2.3 Objection stress test

```
My offer: [describe it, including price]
My buyer: [who]

Play the role of a skeptical buyer who wants this but has not
committed. Give me the 8 objections you would actually raise,
ranked by how likely they are. For each one, write the two-sentence
answer I should have ready. Be harsh. I would rather hear it from
you than lose the sale.
```

### 2.4 Price increase letter

```
I am raising prices from [old] to [new], effective [date].
Client type: [who]
Why: [the honest reason]

Write the email. Rules: no over-explaining, no apology spiral, no
begging. Acknowledge the change, give one honest reason, tell them
exactly what happens next and when, and thank them. Under 150 words.
```

### 2.5 The quiet lead reviver

```
Here is a lead who went cold: [name, what we discussed, how long ago]

Write three different re-engagement emails, sent [1 week], [1 month],
and [3 months] apart. Each one should give them something useful
even if they never reply. Each under 90 words. Nothing needy.
```

### 2.6 Cold outreach that is actually researched

```
Prospect: [company name and what they do]
What I noticed: [paste their site copy, a recent post, a job listing]
What I sell: [one sentence]

Write a cold email under 100 words. Open with the specific thing I
noticed, connect it to a problem they probably have, and make a
small ask (not a 30 minute call, something smaller). Then write two
follow-ups for the same thread.
```

## 3. Customer service and communication

### 3.1 The angry customer reply

```
Here is the message I received: [paste it]
The facts as I know them: [what actually happened]
What I am willing to do: [refund / redo / discount / nothing]

Write a reply that takes responsibility for what is actually mine,
does not grovel, states clearly what I will do, and gives a
deadline. Under 130 words. Warm, not corporate. Do not use the
phrase "we apologize for any inconvenience."
```

### 3.2 Saying no without burning the bridge

```
The request: [what they asked for]
Why I am saying no: [honest reason]
What I can offer instead: [alternative, if any]

Write a short, kind, clear no. No maybe-later hedging. End with
something genuinely useful to them.
```

### 3.3 Build my FAQ from my inbox

```
Here are the last 30 customer questions I received: [paste them]

1. Group them into themes and count how often each theme appears.
2. Write a clear answer for each theme, 60 words max, in plain
   language.
3. Rank them by how much time answering them costs me.
4. Tell me which three should be fixed on my website instead of
   answered in an FAQ.
```

### 3.4 The scheduling and reminder pack

```
My business: [type]
My appointment: [what it is, how long, what they should bring]

Write the full message set: booking confirmation, 48-hour reminder,
2-hour reminder, no-show follow-up, and a post-appointment thank you
with a review request. SMS length for the reminders, email length for
the rest. Warm, short, no exclamation point overload.
```

## 4. Operations and admin

### 4.1 Turn my brain dump into an SOP

```
Here is how I do [task], described badly and out of order:
[brain dump]

Turn it into a written SOP with: purpose, when to run it, tools
needed, numbered steps with the actual clicks, common mistakes,
and a done-checklist. Write it so somebody on day one could follow
it without asking me a question. Flag anything ambiguous rather
than guessing.
```

### 4.2 The weekly reset

```
Here is everything on my plate right now: [dump the whole list]
My revenue goal this quarter: [goal]
Hours I actually have this week: [number]

Sort every item into: does the needle move, keeps the lights on,
feels productive but is not, or should be deleted. Then build me a
realistic week. Protect [number] hours for [the one thing that
actually grows the business]. Tell me plainly what I am not doing
this week.
```

### 4.3 Find what I should automate

```
Here is my week, task by task, with rough time spent: [list]

For each task, score: frequency, time cost, how repeatable it is,
and how much judgment it needs. Then rank the top 5 automation
candidates and describe what the automated version looks like in
practice. Be honest about which ones are not worth automating.
```

### 4.4 Vendor and tool comparison

```
I need software for [job to be done].
Budget: [amount per month]
Must have: [non-negotiables]
Nice to have: [list]
My constraints: [team size, technical skill, existing tools]

Give me a comparison table of 5 real options with pricing, the
single best reason to pick each, and the deal-breaker for each.
Then a one-line recommendation for someone in my exact situation.
Flag anything where your pricing info might be out of date so I
can check it.
```

### 4.5 Meeting notes to actions

```
Here is the transcript or my notes: [paste]

Output three things:
1. Decisions made (with who decided)
2. Action items, as a table: task, owner, due date, blocker
3. Open questions nobody answered

Then write a 5-line recap email I can send to everyone who was
in the room.
```

## 5. Money and numbers

### 5.1 Explain my own numbers to me

```
Here are my numbers for [period]: [paste revenue, expenses, whatever
you have, even if it is messy]

1. Tell me what actually happened in plain language, no accounting
   jargon.
2. Show the three biggest expense categories as a percentage of
   revenue.
3. Flag anything that looks off or worth a second look.
4. Give me three questions I should be able to answer about this
   and cannot yet.
```

### 5.2 Should I raise my prices

```
Current price: [price]
What it costs me to deliver (time and money): [details]
Close rate: [percentage, or "almost everyone says yes"]
Competitors charge: [range]
My capacity: [full / room to spare]

Walk me through whether I should raise prices, what to, and how to
phase it. Show your reasoning. If the answer is no, say no.
```

### 5.3 The pricing tier builder

```
My service: [what it is]
Everything I could include: [list every deliverable]
Cost to deliver each: [rough time or money]
Buyer types: [describe 2 or 3]

Build three tiers. Name them in plain language, price them, and
make the middle tier the obvious pick. For each tier tell me who
it is for in one line and what deliberately got left out.
```

### 5.4 Cash flow reality check

```
Money in the bank: [amount]
Money coming in, with dates: [list]
Money going out, with dates: [list]
Upcoming known costs: [list]

Build me a 13-week cash flow table. Mark the tightest week. Tell me
the two levers that would help most, and the earliest date I could
safely spend [amount] on [the thing you want to buy].
```

## 6. Hiring and delegating

### 6.1 Write the job post

```
Role: [title]
What they will actually do all day: [be specific]
Must have: [skills]
Do not care about: [degree, years, whatever you genuinely do not care about]
Pay: [range]
Setup: [remote / hybrid / hours]

Write a job post that a real person would want to answer. Plain
language, no "rockstar," no "wear many hats" unless we mean it and
say why. Include a short application task that filters for the skill
that actually matters.
```

### 6.2 First 30 days plan

```
New hire role: [title]
What I need them owning by day 30: [outcome]
What I have documented already: [list, honestly]

Build a week-by-week onboarding plan. Week 1 through week 4. For each
week: what they learn, what they do, and what "good" looks like at
the end of it. Flag every gap where I need to write documentation
before day one.
```

### 6.3 The delegation brief

```
Task I am handing off: [task]
How I currently do it: [describe]
What good looks like: [standard]
What they can decide alone: [list]
What they must check with me on: [list]

Write a one-page handoff brief. Include the definition of done, the
three most likely mistakes, and where the passwords or files live
(leave placeholders, do not invent).
```

## 7. Product and offers

### 7.1 Pressure test my idea

```
My idea: [describe it]
Who it is for: [audience]
What they do today instead: [the current workaround]
What I would charge: [price]

Be a skeptical advisor, not a cheerleader. Answer:
- Who specifically feels this pain enough to pay today
- Why the current workaround might be good enough
- The three riskiest assumptions I am making
- The cheapest test that would kill the idea in under two weeks
- What you would build first if you had one weekend

If this is a bad idea, say so directly.
```

### 7.2 The smallest sellable version

```
The full vision: [describe everything you want it to be]
Time I have: [realistic hours]
Skills I have: [list]

Cut this down to the smallest version that a real person would pay
for. Tell me exactly what gets built, what gets faked manually
behind the scenes, and what gets cut entirely. Then give me a build
order where I can stop after any step and still have something
sellable.
```

### 7.3 Name it

```
What it is: [describe]
Who it is for: [audience]
The feeling I want: [three words]
Names I already rejected and why: [list]

Give me 20 names in four buckets: plain and descriptive, short and
punchy, slightly unexpected, and made-up-but-pronounceable. For each,
give the .com odds honestly and a one-line tagline. Skip anything
that sounds like a pharmaceutical.
```

### 7.4 The onboarding sequence

```
Product: [what they bought]
First win I want them to have: [the smallest success]
Time to that win: [realistic]
Where people usually get stuck: [list]

Write a 5-email onboarding sequence. Email 1 immediately, then
day 1, day 3, day 7, day 14. Each one has a single job. Include
subject lines. Keep them short, people do not read onboarding
emails, they skim them.
```

## 8. Content and thought leadership

### 8.1 Find my content angles

```
What I do: [business]
What I know that most people in my field do not: [list, be honest]
Questions customers ask me constantly: [list]
Opinions I hold that others would argue with: [list]

Give me 20 content ideas grouped by: teaching, opinion, story, and
behind the scenes. For each, write the hook line only. Rank by which
would be easiest for me specifically to make this week.
```

### 8.2 Interview me into a blog post

```
Topic: [what you want to write about]

Interview me. Ask one question at a time, wait for my answer, and
follow up on anything vague. After 8 to 10 questions, write the post
using my words wherever possible. 700 to 900 words, subheadings,
no fluff intro. Do not use em dashes.
```

### 8.3 Repurpose the long thing

```
Here is my [blog post / video transcript / newsletter]: [paste]

Turn it into:
- 5 social posts, each standing alone
- 1 email to my list, under 200 words
- 3 short quotes worth putting on a graphic
- 1 FAQ entry
- 5 replies I could leave on other people's posts about this topic

Keep my voice. Do not add claims that were not in the original.
```

## 9. The meta prompts (use these on everything else)

### 9.1 Make my prompt better

```
Here is a prompt I wrote: [paste it]
What I actually want: [describe the outcome]

Rewrite my prompt so it produces a better result. Add the role,
context, format, and constraints I forgot. Then explain in three
bullets what you changed and why, so I get better at this.
```

### 9.2 The critique pass

```
Here is your last answer: [paste it]

Now critique it hard. What is generic? What did you assume? What
would an expert in this field push back on? Then give me a better
version that fixes what you found.
```

### 9.3 Teach it my voice

```
Here are five things I have written: [paste them]

Analyze my voice: sentence length and rhythm, vocabulary I reach for,
words I never use, punctuation habits, how I open, how I close, how
I handle humor. Write it up as a voice guide I can paste at the top
of future prompts. Then use it to write [thing] so I can check
whether you got it.
```

### 9.4 The reality check

```
Here is what you just told me: [paste]

Which parts of that are you confident about, and which parts are
you guessing? Mark every factual claim as verified, likely, or
uncertain. For anything uncertain, tell me exactly what I should
check and where.
```

## The prompt formula (for when you need one that is not in here)

```
You are [role with specific experience].

Context:
[Everything true about my situation. More is better. Paste the mess.]

Task:
[The single thing you want. One task per prompt.]

Format:
[Exactly what the output should look like. Length, structure, shape.]

Constraints:
[What to avoid. Tone. Words you hate. No em dashes.]

Before you answer, ask me up to five questions you need answered
to do this well.
```

Write it once, save it, reuse it forever. That is the whole trick.

---

## One honest warning

AI will confidently hand you a wrong number, a fake statistic, or a legal opinion it made up on the spot. Use it for drafting, structuring, summarizing, and rewriting. Verify anything factual, financial, or legal with a human who is liable for being right.

Use it as the assistant you cannot afford to hire yet. Not as the expert you still need.
