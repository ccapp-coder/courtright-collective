# Founder's Copy Bank

## Start here

Here's the thing about writing your own marketing copy. You are too close to it. You have been staring at your product for six months, you know every feature, and the second you sit down to describe it you either write a novel or you write "we help businesses grow."

This is the fix. Every line below is a real, usable piece of copy with the specific bits swapped out for brackets. Fill in the brackets. Say it out loud. If it sounds like you, ship it.

**Three rules for using this thing:**

1. **Fill the brackets with specifics, not adjectives.** `[result]` is not "better results." It is "17 hours a month back" or "three fewer no-shows a week."
2. **Cut a third.** Whatever you write, delete a third of it. Your copy gets better every time.
3. **Read it out loud.** If you would not say it to a friend at a bar, rewrite it.

**How to import to Notion:** Notion, then `Import`, then `Markdown`, then drop this file in. Every section becomes a toggle-friendly heading you can duplicate per campaign.

## 1. Headlines that do the job

### Outcome headlines (the safest bet)

- `[Outcome]` without `[the annoying thing]`.
- Get `[specific result]` in `[timeframe]`. No `[common objection]`.
- The `[category]` that actually `[does the thing people wish it did]`.
- Stop `[painful task]`. Start `[desired state]`.
- `[Number]` `[unit of work]` back every `[week/month]`.
- Everything you need to `[job to be done]`. Nothing you don't.
- Your `[thing]`, handled.
- `[Do the hard thing]` in an afternoon.
- Built for `[specific person]`, not `[the generic crowd]`.
- From `[bad current state]` to `[good future state]` in `[timeframe]`.

### Contrarian headlines (use one, not five)

- You don't need `[expensive thing everyone recommends]`. You need `[your thing]`.
- `[Common advice]` is fine. It is also why `[bad outcome]` keeps happening.
- Most `[category]` tools are built for `[big companies]`. This one isn't.
- We are not the cheapest `[category]`. Here is why people pick us anyway.
- The boring version of `[trendy thing]` that actually works.

### Question headlines (best for ads and landing page sections)

- Still `[doing the manual thing]` by hand?
- What would you do with `[reclaimed resource]` a week?
- How much is `[the problem]` costing you right now?
- Ready to `[goal]` but not ready to `[expensive step]`?

### Direct headlines (best for checkout and offer pages)

- `[Product name]`. `[What it is]` for `[who]`.
- `[Price]`. `[What they get]`. `[How fast]`.
- One `[deliverable]`. `[Timeframe]`. `[Price]`.

## 2. Subheads that keep them reading

- We build `[the thing]` so you can get back to `[what they actually want to do]`.
- No `[dreaded thing]`. No `[other dreaded thing]`. Just `[the deliverable]`.
- For `[specific person]` who `[specific situation]`.
- You tell us the outcome. We figure out how to get there.
- Set it up once. It runs whether you remember it or not.
- If you have ever said "I should really `[task]`" and then not done it, this is for you.
- Everything below is included. There is no upsell hiding at the end.
- Takes about `[realistic time]` to set up. Then it just runs.

## 3. The "what we do" paragraph

Pick a lane. Fill it in. Use it on your homepage, your email signature, your LinkedIn, your pitch.

**The short one (one line, for bios and signatures):**

> I help `[who]` `[do what]` without `[the painful part]`.

**The medium one (for homepage hero and About sections):**

> `[Business name]` is a `[what kind of business]` for `[specific audience]`. We `[core service]` so `[audience]` can `[the outcome they want]`. Most people come to us because `[the trigger moment]`. They stay because `[the reason people stay]`.

**The long one (for About pages and proposals):**

> Here's the short version. `[Business name]` started because `[honest origin, one sentence]`. We kept running into the same problem: `[the problem]`. So we built `[the solution]`.
>
> Today we work with `[audience]` on `[service one]`, `[service two]`, and `[service three]`. We are `[size/structure honest detail]`, which means you are talking to the person who does the work. Not an account manager, not a queue.
>
> If your `[business]` is at the point where `[the trigger]`, we should talk.

**The one-liner formula bank:**

- We `[verb]` `[thing]` for `[audience]`.
- `[Product]` turns `[messy input]` into `[clean output]`.
- Think `[familiar thing]`, but for `[your niche]`.
- The `[deliverable]` you keep meaning to make, made.

## 4. Offer and pricing copy

### Framing the price

- `[Price]`. That's `[comparison that makes it feel small]`.
- Less than `[relatable spend]` a month.
- One `[unit of their revenue]` pays for the whole thing.
- If it saves you `[time]` a month, it pays for itself `[X]` times over.
- Flat rate. No hourly billing, no surprise invoice at the end.
- You will know the total before we start. It will not change.

### Package name patterns

- **Starter / Growth / Full Stack**
- **The Basics / The Works / The Whole Thing**
- **One-Time Setup / Done For You / Done and Maintained**
- **Try It / Run It / Scale It**

### Package descriptions

> **`[Tier one]`, `[price]`.** Best for `[situation]`. You get `[deliverable]`, `[deliverable]`, and `[deliverable]`. Live in about `[timeframe]`.
>
> **`[Tier two]`, `[price]`.** Best for `[situation]`. Everything above plus `[deliverable]` and `[deliverable]`. This is what most people pick.
>
> **`[Tier three]`, `[price]`.** Best for `[situation]`. Everything above plus `[deliverable]`, `[deliverable]`, and monthly check-ins where we adjust as things change.

### Guarantee lines

- If `[the thing]` doesn't `[do the thing]` in `[timeframe]`, you don't pay. Simple as that.
- `[X]`-day window. If it isn't working for you, email me and I refund it. No form, no hoops.
- I would rather refund you than have you tell someone this was a waste.

### Scarcity that is not fake

- I take `[number]` of these a month because I do the work myself.
- Next opening is `[month]`.
- Price goes to `[higher price]` on `[date]`. If you are in before then, you keep this rate.

- Send me the `[deliverable]`
- Show me how it works
- Grab it for `[price]`

### CTA sentences

- Book a free `[length]` call. Worst case, you leave with a plan you can run yourself.
- Grab it, use it today, tell me if it helped.
- One click, then we pick a time.
- No pitch deck. Just `[length]` and a straight answer about whether we can help.
- If it isn't a fit, I will say so and point you somewhere better.

## 6. Email copy

### Cold outreach (short version, the one that works)

> Subject: quick question about `[specific thing you noticed]`
>
> Hey `[name]`,
>
> Saw `[specific, real detail about their business]`. Nice work on `[genuine thing]`.
>
> Quick question. Are you still handling `[task]` manually? I ask because I build `[solution]` for `[their type of business]`, and that one usually eats `[realistic time]` a week.
>
> If it is worth `[length]` to you, I will show you what it would look like for `[their business]`. No pitch, no deck.
>
> Best,
> `[Your name]`

### Follow-up one (three days later)

> Subject: re: quick question about `[thing]`
>
> Hey `[name]`,
>
> Following up on this one. Totally fine if the timing is wrong, I just did not want it to get buried.
>
> If `[task]` is not the pain point, what is? I am nosy about this stuff.
>
> Best,
> `[Your name]`

### Follow-up two (the polite close)

> Subject: closing the loop
>
> Hey `[name]`,
>
> Last one from me, promise. I will get out of your inbox.
>
> If `[the problem]` comes back around, my door is open. Here is `[free resource]` either way, no strings.
>
> Best,
> `[Your name]`

### Welcome email (after they buy)

> Subject: You're in. Here's what happens next.
>
> Hey `[name]`,
>
> Thanks for grabbing `[product]`. Genuinely, it means a lot.
>
> Here is what happens now:
>
> 1. `[First step, with timeframe]`
> 2. `[Second step, with timeframe]`
> 3. `[Third step, with timeframe]`
>
> If you get stuck anywhere, reply to this email. It comes straight to me.
>
> Best,
> `[Your name]`

### Launch email

> Subject: `[Product]` is live
>
> Hey `[name]`,
>
> `[Product]` is live. `[One sentence on what it does.]`
>
> I built it because `[honest reason]`. Kept running into `[problem]` and got tired of the workaround.
>
> Here's what is inside:
>
> - `[Thing one]`
> - `[Thing two]`
> - `[Thing three]`
>
> `[Price]`, and it is yours forever. `[Link]`
>
> Best,
> `[Your name]`

### Re-engagement / win-back

> Subject: still `[doing the thing]`?
>
> Hey `[name]`,
>
> It has been a while. We talked about `[topic]` back in `[month]` and I am curious whether you ever got it sorted.
>
> If yes, I would love to hear what worked. If no, I have `[new thing]` now that handles exactly that.
>
> Either way, hope `[their business]` is treating you well.
>
> Best,
> `[Your name]`

### The invoice / payment nudge (nobody likes writing this one)

> Subject: invoice `[number]`
>
> Hey `[name]`,
>
> Quick nudge on invoice `[number]` for `[amount]`, dated `[date]`. Link is here: `[link]`
>
> If something is holding it up on your end, just tell me and we will work it out. No stress.
>
> Best,
> `[Your name]`

### Asking for a testimonial

> Subject: 60 seconds, if you have them
>
> Hey `[name]`,
>
> Glad `[the thing]` is working out. Would you be up for a couple of sentences I could put on the site?
>
> To make it easy, you could just answer these:
>
> 1. What was going on before we started?
> 2. What changed?
> 3. Would you tell someone else to do it?
>
> Reply in whatever form is easiest. Voice memo works too.
>
> Best,
> `[Your name]`

### Asking for a referral

> Subject: who else should I be talking to?
>
> Hey `[name]`,
>
> Random question. Do you know anybody else running a `[type of business]` who is drowning in `[the problem]`?
>
> I have room for `[number]` more `[clients/projects]` this `[quarter]`, and honestly most of my good work comes from people like you pointing me somewhere.
>
> No pressure at all if nobody comes to mind.
>
> Best,
> `[Your name]`

## 7. Social captions

### Launch

> `[Product]` is live. `[One sentence on what it does.]` Built it because `[honest reason]`. Go break it and tell me what needs fixing. 🙌

### Behind the scenes

> Spent `[time]` today on `[specific unglamorous task]`. Nobody sees this part. It is also the part that makes `[the visible part]` work.

### Milestone

> `[The number or the milestone]`. Took `[honest timeframe]` to get here. Thanks to `[specific people]` for `[specific help]`. On to the next one.

### Teaching (the highest-value post you can make)

> Three things I got wrong about `[topic]`:
>
> 1. `[Wrong belief]`. What actually works: `[the real thing]`.
> 2. `[Wrong belief]`. What actually works: `[the real thing]`.
> 3. `[Wrong belief]`. What actually works: `[the real thing]`.
>
> Save this one for later.

### Customer win

> `[Customer type]` came to me with `[problem]`. We `[what you did]`. Now `[specific result]`. That is the whole job. 🎯

### Soft promo

> If you have said "I should really `[task]`" more than twice this month, that is your sign. I do this for `[audience]`. Link in bio.

### Hard promo (use sparingly)

> `[Product]`. `[Price]`. `[What you get, in three words]`. `[Link]`

### The reply-bait question

> Honest question for `[audience]`: what is the one task you would pay to never do again? I will go first. `[Yours.]`

## 8. Objection handlers

Keep these in a doc. Paste them into DMs and sales calls without rewriting them every time.

**"It's too expensive."**

> Totally fair. Here is the math I would run in your shoes: `[task]` takes about `[time]` a `[period]`. At `[their rate]`, that is `[cost]` a year. This is `[price]` once. If the math still does not work, it does not work, and I would rather tell you that than sell you something.

**"I need to think about it."**

> Of course. What is the piece you are unsure about? If it is `[common concern]`, I can answer that in a sentence. If it is timing, tell me when to check back and I will actually check back.

**"We already have someone doing this."**

> Good, that is one less thing on your plate. Is it working the way you want? If yes, ignore me completely. If there is a gap, I am happy to fill just the gap rather than replace anybody.

**"How do I know it will work?"**

> You don't, not for certain, and I am not going to pretend otherwise. What I can do is show you `[proof/case/example]` and start with `[the smallest version]` so you are risking `[small amount]` instead of `[big amount]`.

**"Can you do it cheaper?"**

> I can do it smaller. Same rate, less scope. Tell me which piece matters most and we will start there.

**"I'll do it myself."**

> Honestly, you probably can. Here is `[free resource]` to get you started. If you get three weeks in and it is still on the list, come find me.

## 9. Trust builders

### Proof lines

- `[Number]` `[businesses/projects/customers]` since `[year]`.
- `[Specific result]` for `[type of client]` in `[timeframe]`.
- Been doing this since `[year]`. Still answer my own emails.
- `[Recognizable proof point: an app in the store, a client logo, a number]`.

### Risk reversal

- You are not signing a contract. You can stop any month.
- We start with `[small paid step]` so you can see how I work before committing to the big one.
- Everything I build, you own. Accounts in your name, files in your drive.

### Honest disclaimers (these build more trust than they cost)

- This will not `[thing it does not do]`. If that is what you need, `[alternative]` is better for it.
- Best fit for `[situation]`. Worst fit for `[situation]`.
- This takes work on your end too. About `[realistic time]` from you.

## 11. Fill-in formulas (when you are stuck at a blank page)

| Formula | Shape | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PAS | Problem, Agitate, Solve | Sales pages, cold email |
| BAB | Before, After, Bridge | Social posts, case studies |
| The One-Liner | For `[who]` who `[problem]`, `[product]` is `[category]` that `[benefit]` | Bios, pitches, elevator answers |
| FAB | Feature, Advantage, Benefit | Product pages, spec lists |
| 4 Ps | Promise, Picture, Proof, Push | Long-form sales pages |
| The Rule of One | One reader, one idea, one promise, one action | Every email you ever send |

**Worked example, BAB:**

> Before: `[Their Monday morning today, in one specific sentence.]`
> After: `[Their Monday morning after, in one specific sentence.]`
> Bridge: `[Your product, in one sentence, plus the CTA.]`

## 12. The words to cut

Search your draft for these and delete or replace every one.

| Cut this | Because | Say this instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Game-changer | Means nothing | The specific change |
| Unlock your potential | Fortune cookie | The actual outcome |
| Revolutionary | You are not | New, or nothing |
| Seamless | Everyone says it | Takes 4 minutes |
| Solutions | Vague | The actual thing you deliver |
| Leverage | Corporate | Use |
| Utilize | Corporate | Use |
| Best-in-class | Unprovable | The proof point |
| Cutting-edge | Aging badly | What it does |
| Synergy | No | Delete the sentence |
| We're passionate about | Everyone claims it | Show it instead |
| In today's fast-paced world | Delete on sight | Start at sentence two |

And one formatting rule that will make your copy read human instantly: **no em dashes**. Use a period, a comma, a set of parentheses, or an ellipsis. Em dashes are the fastest way to make good writing look machine-made.

## 13. The 15-minute copy sprint

When you have to write something and you have no time:

1. **Minute 0 to 2.** Write the one sentence you would say out loud if a friend asked what this is.
2. **Minute 2 to 5.** Pick a headline from Section 1. Fill the brackets with your specifics.
3. **Minute 5 to 9.** Write three bullets. What they get, how fast, what it costs.
4. **Minute 9 to 12.** Add one proof line from Section 9 and one objection handler from Section 8.
5. **Minute 12 to 14.** Pick a CTA from Section 5.
6. **Minute 14 to 15.** Read the whole thing out loud. Cut a third. Ship it.

Done beats perfect, and shipped copy you can edit next week beats brilliant copy that never leaves your drafts.
