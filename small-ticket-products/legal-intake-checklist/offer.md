## Sales blurb

**A small firm loses more revenue to slow intake than to bad marketing.**

Somebody calls on a Tuesday afternoon. Nobody picks up, because everyone is in court or on a call or eating lunch for the first time that day. They leave a voicemail. It gets returned Thursday. By Thursday they have already talked to two other firms and hired one of them.

The marketing worked. The phone rang. The case went somewhere else anyway.

That is an operations problem, not a legal one. Which is good news, because operations problems have checklists.

**Seven stages, each with a finish line and a time target:**

1. **First contact.** Every entry point covered, phone through chat, plus the auto-reply that buys you time without creating a relationship you did not intend to create.
2. **The screening call.** A word-for-word opening script, the fifteen things to capture, five qualifying questions, and eight red flags worth naming out loud. One flag is a conversation. Two is usually a decline.
3. **Conflicts check.** The full search list, including the part most firms skip: logging the prospective clients you declined, because those create conflicts too.
4. **The consultation.** Complete intake form field list, the before-during checklist, and the fee conversation written out so you can say the number out loud and then stop talking. Filling that silence is how firms discount work nobody asked them to discount.
5. **Engagement.** Thirteen-point agreement checklist and the 24-hour rule, because delay is where signatures die.
6. **Onboarding.** The first 24 hours, plus the welcome email that reduces client anxiety and cuts the "any update?" calls.
7. **Closing the loop.** Declines, non-retains, referral thank-yous, and a decline letter that protects everybody.

Plus **the seven metrics to track monthly** with healthy targets, and **the six things to automate first,** ranked by return.

**$29. Print it, put it next to whoever answers the phone.**

> If your speed to first response is over an hour, fix that before you spend another dollar on marketing. It is the cheapest revenue increase available to your firm and it does not require one new lead. That is the most valuable sentence in the whole checklist and it is on page one.

## Notes and assumptions

- **Ambiguity call, noted as instructed.** "Legal intake checklist" could have meant either a law firm's client intake process or a legal-basics checklist for business owners. I built the law firm version, for two reasons. First, "Client-Portal Demo" is also in this product library, and the two together make a coherent law firm vertical: a $29 checklist that ends by naming the automations, then a portal demo, then a build. Second, solo attorneys and small firms buy operational tools readily and are an excellent services client. If the other reading was intended, it is a separate product and worth building as one.
- Priced at $29. Attorneys do not blink at $29 and it prices as a professional tool rather than a freebie.
- **Carefully bounded on liability.** It says up front and again at the end that this is an operations checklist, not legal advice, that jurisdiction rules of professional conduct govern and win any disagreement, and that engagement documents need review by licensed counsel. It never drafts contract language and never opines on the substance of any rule.
- Best upsell path: Checklist, then Get-Found-on-Google Mini-Audit or Client-Portal Demo, then Email Automation and Website Creation.
- Natural follow-on product if this performs: the same checklist structure for medical, dental, and home services practices. The intake problem is identical in every appointment-based business.
