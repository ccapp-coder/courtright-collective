## Sales blurb

**The reason most people never ship an app is not talent, and it is not time.**

It is that they have no process. So every decision is a fresh decision, and fresh decisions at 11pm are how projects die.

This is my process. Six phases, each with a real finish line. Nights and weekends, kids asleep, day job still happening. When I am tired and I do not know what to do next, I open this and do the next thing on the list. That is the whole trick. There is no other trick.

**The six phases:**

1. **Pick something small enough to finish.** Six scoping questions with honest scoring, plus the full list of things that felt essential and were not (accounts, onboarding, dark mode, Android at launch) and the three things that genuinely were.
2. **Validate in two weeks, max.** Including the single highest-value hour in the entire process: reading the one and two star reviews of your top five competitors. Plus written kill criteria, so you cannot move the goalposts on yourself later.
3. **Build the thing.** The build order that keeps you sane, the six rules that saved me the most time, and the one habit worth more than every productivity system I have tried: write down the exact next step before you close the laptop.
4. **The submission gauntlet.** Complete pre-submit checklist for accounts, legal, app, and listing. Plus the five rejections you will probably get and the actual fix for each one.
5. **Launch.** Why Tuesday and not Friday, the two-week prep list, launch day in order, and an honest description of what launch day actually looks like when you have no audience yet.
6. **The part everyone skips.** The first 30 days, the maintenance rhythm, and how to know when an app has earned the right to be killed.

Every phase ends with a checklist. The last page is the whole thing on one page: phase, realistic timeline, finish line.

**$27. Roughly 12 to 16 weeks of nights, mapped out.**

> No hype in here. Launch day for an indie app with no audience is quiet, dozens of downloads and not thousands, and the playbook says so instead of selling you a fantasy. The growth comes in month three, from search traffic and updates and replying to every single review.
