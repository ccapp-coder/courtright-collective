# The Social Starter Pack

## The actual problem

You are not bad at social media. You are trying to invent content at 9pm on a Tuesday with no plan, which is a task nobody is good at.

Here's the thing. Marketing teams do not sit down and think of a post. They work off pillars and formats decided weeks ago, so the daily job is filling in a shape rather than inventing one. That is the only real difference between them and you, and it is a system you can copy in an afternoon.

This pack is that system: four pillars, a 30-day plan, ninety caption templates, and a batching workflow that takes about two hours a month.

**One thing before we start.** Posting daily is not the goal. Three good posts a week, forever, beats twenty posts in January and silence until June. Pick a pace you can hold on your worst week, not your best one.

---

## Part 1: The four pillars

Every post you make should be one of these four. That is it. When you know the pillar, the post writes itself.

### Pillar 1: Teach (about 40 percent of your posts)

Show that you know your work. The single most underrated content type for a small business, because it builds trust with people who are not ready to buy yet, which is most people.

- Three things people get wrong about `[your field]`
- How to tell if you need `[your service]` or not
- What that thing costs and why
- The mistake you see every week
- A question you get asked constantly, answered properly
- Show a decision: two options, and how you pick between them

### Pillar 2: Show (about 30 percent)

Behind the scenes. Process. The unglamorous parts. This is the pillar that makes you a person rather than a logo.

- Before and after
- The setup at 6am
- A time-lapse of the boring part
- Your actual workspace, not a staged one
- A tool you love and why
- What today looked like

### Pillar 3: Prove (about 20 percent)

Evidence that you are good at this. Not bragging, just showing the work.

- A finished job, with what the problem was
- A customer's own words, screenshotted
- A number: years in business, jobs completed, this month's total
- A hard problem you solved
- A review, with a thank you

### Pillar 4: Ask (about 10 percent)

Actually ask for the business. Most small businesses either never do this or do only this. Both are wrong.

- What you do, plainly, with a link
- An opening in your schedule
- A seasonal offer
- A soft one: "if you have been meaning to `[thing]`, now is the time"

**Why the percentages matter:** at 10 percent asking, every ask lands with people who already trust you. At 80 percent asking, nobody is listening when it counts.

## Part 2: Your 30-day plan

Three posts a week. Monday, Wednesday, Friday. Twelve posts, one month, no thinking required.

| Day | Pillar | The post |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 (Mon) | Teach | The single most common question you get, answered properly |
| 3 (Wed) | Show | Your workspace or setup, as it actually looks |
| 5 (Fri) | Prove | A recent job, with the problem and the result |
| 8 (Mon) | Teach | Three things people get wrong about your field |
| 10 (Wed) | Show | The unglamorous part nobody sees |
| 12 (Fri) | Ask | What you do and who it is for, plainly, with a link |
| 15 (Mon) | Teach | What it costs, and what changes the price |
| 17 (Wed) | Show | A tool or a technique you rely on |
| 19 (Fri) | Prove | A customer's words, screenshotted, with a thank you |
| 22 (Mon) | Teach | How to tell whether you need this or not |
| 24 (Wed) | Show | Before and after |
| 26 (Fri) | Prove | A number that says something. Years, jobs, this month |

Day 29 is a bonus if you have the energy: a question post that invites replies. Comments are the cheapest reach there is.

**Then start over.** Month two uses the same twelve shapes with different content. You will run out of months before you run out of shapes.

## Part 3: Batching, two hours a month

Doing this daily is what kills people. Do it in one sitting instead.

### The two-hour session, once a month

**First 30 minutes: gather.**
Go through your camera roll from the last month. Pull every usable photo and clip into one folder. You already have more content than you think, and it is better than anything you would stage.

**Next 30 minutes: sort.**
Assign each photo to one of the twelve slots above. Some slots will have nothing. Note them, and take those photos this week. You now have a shot list instead of a vague obligation.

**Next 45 minutes: write.**
Twelve captions, using the templates in `Caption-Templates.md`. Do not polish. Fill in the shapes, move on. First drafts posted beat perfect drafts unposted, every single time.

**Last 15 minutes: schedule.**
Load them into whatever scheduler you use. Meta Business Suite is free and covers Facebook and Instagram. Buffer's free tier covers three channels. Native scheduling exists in most apps now.

Then close the laptop and do not think about social media for four weeks.

### The rule that makes batching work

**Take photos constantly, write captions never.** Photos are the perishable part, and you can only take them in the moment. Captions can be written any time. So all month, your only job is to point your phone at things. The writing happens in one sitting.

## Part 4: Fixing your profile first

Do this once, today, before you post anything else. A good post on a bad profile is wasted.

### The bio formula

```
[What you do] for [who you do it for].
[The thing that makes you different, or a proof point.]
[Where you are.] [Call to action.]
[Link]
```

Worked example:

> Plumbing for Nashville homeowners.
> Family run since 2011. Same-day emergency calls.
> East Nashville. Book below.
> nashvilleplumbing.com/book

### Profile checklist

- [ ] Profile photo is your logo, or your face if you are the brand. Not a stock image
- [ ] It is recognizable as a tiny circle. Check it at actual size
- [ ] Bio says what you do in the first four words
- [ ] Bio says who it is for
- [ ] Location is in there, if you serve a local area
- [ ] The link works and goes somewhere useful, not just the homepage
- [ ] Contact button set up: call, email, or directions
- [ ] Business category set correctly
- [ ] Hours are accurate, especially over holidays
- [ ] Handle is the same everywhere you can get it
- [ ] Highlights or featured posts cover: what you do, proof, and how to book

### The link question

Send people to one useful page, not your homepage. A booking page, a menu, a quote form, a specific offer. Link-in-bio tools are fine, but a single direct link converts better than a menu of five.

## Part 5: Platform notes

You do not need to be everywhere. **Pick one, maybe two.** A business with one good account beats one with four abandoned ones.

| Platform | Best for | Post what | How often |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Instagram** | Anything visual. Food, trades, retail, before-and-afters | Photos, short vertical video, carousels | 3 a week |
| **Facebook** | Local services, older customers, community groups | The same posts as Instagram, plus local group participation | 3 a week |
| **LinkedIn** | B2B, professional services, consultants | Teaching posts, opinions, plain text | 2 a week |
| **TikTok** | Reaching people who do not know you exist | Short vertical video with a hook in 2 seconds | Only if you like making video |
| **Nextdoor** | Hyper-local services | Answer neighbor questions, do not advertise | Weekly |
| **YouTube** | Long-form how-to that ranks in search for years | Anything you can explain in 8 minutes | Monthly |

**How to choose:** where are your customers already, and which format can you make without hating your life? Both answers have to be yes.

### The stuff that is true everywhere

- **The first line is the whole job.** People decide in one second. Write the first line last, when you know what the post says
- **One idea per post.** Not three
- **Vertical video outperforms everything right now** on Instagram, Facebook, and TikTok. That will change. It has not changed yet
- **Reply to every comment,** especially in the first hour. It is the cheapest reach available
- **Post when your people are awake,** not when a blog told you. Your own analytics know better than any article
- **Hashtags are close to dead as a discovery mechanism.** Three to five relevant ones, not thirty. Nobody has been helped by a wall of hashtags since about 2019

## Part 6: What to measure

Almost every metric you can see is noise. Three are not.

| Metric | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| **Saves and shares** | Somebody found it useful enough to keep or pass on. The single best content signal there is |
| **Profile visits** | They liked the post enough to check who you are. That is intent |
| **Link clicks or DMs** | The only thing that turns into money |

**Ignore:** follower count, likes, impressions, and "reach" on its own. A thousand followers who never buy is a vanity number. Forty local followers who do is a business.

Check once a month, not daily. Look at your top three posts, ask what they had in common, and make more of that. That is the whole analytics practice for a business your size.

---

## Part 7: The honest expectations

**Month 1.** Almost nothing happens. This is normal and it is where most people quit.

**Month 2 or 3.** Somebody mentions they saw your post. That is the first real signal.

**Month 4 to 6.** You start getting the occasional message or booking that traces back to social. Small, but real.

**Month 6+.** It compounds, quietly. Not because any one post took off, but because you have become a business people have seen before.

Social media for a small business is not a lottery ticket, it is a slow familiarity machine. Three posts a week for six months beats a viral video and then nothing, because the viral video brings you strangers and the six months brings you neighbors.

## Your first hour

1. Fix your bio using the formula in Part 4. Ten minutes
2. Run the profile checklist. Fifteen minutes
3. Open your camera roll and pull twelve usable photos. Fifteen minutes
4. Write three captions from `Caption-Templates.md`. Fifteen minutes
5. Post one. Right now. Today. Five minutes

Then put a recurring two-hour block on your calendar for the first Monday of every month, and call it done.
