## Sales blurb

**You are not bad at social media. You are trying to invent content at 9pm on a Tuesday with no plan.**

Nobody is good at that. Here's the thing: marketing teams do not sit down and think of a post. They work off pillars and formats decided weeks ago, so the daily job is filling in a shape instead of inventing one. That is the entire difference between them and you, and you can copy it in an afternoon.

**What is in the pack:**

- **Four content pillars** with percentages. Teach 40, Show 30, Prove 20, Ask 10. The percentages are the point: at 10 percent asking, every ask lands with people who already trust you. At 80 percent asking, nobody is listening when it counts.
- **A 30-day plan.** Twelve posts, Monday, Wednesday, Friday, each one a specific shape rather than a vague theme. Then you start over with the same twelve shapes and different content. You will run out of months before you run out of shapes.
- **90 caption templates,** sorted by pillar. Myth corrections, straight answers, lists that get saved, behind the scenes, before and afters, job stories, customer words, numbers, plain asks, soft asks, and ten engagement posts for when you want comments.
- **The two-hour batching session.** Gather from your camera roll, sort into slots, write twelve captions, schedule, close the laptop for four weeks. Plus the rule that makes it work: take photos constantly, write captions never. Photos are the perishable part.
- **The profile fix,** with a bio formula and an eleven-point checklist. Do this before you post anything else, because a good post on a bad profile is wasted.
- **Platform notes** that tell you to pick one, maybe two. A business with one good account beats one with four abandoned ones.
- **The three metrics that are not noise** (saves and shares, profile visits, link clicks) and the four that are (followers, likes, impressions, reach).
- **A CSV calendar** with all twelve slots pre-filled: pillar, what to post, what photo you need, and which caption template to use.

**$29. Two hours a month. That is the whole commitment.**

> Honest expectations, which the pack states out loud: month one, almost nothing happens, and that is where most people quit. Month three, somebody mentions they saw your post. Month six, it compounds quietly. Social media for a small business is not a lottery ticket, it is a slow familiarity machine. Three posts a week for six months beats a viral video and then silence, because the viral video brings you strangers and the six months brings you neighbors.
