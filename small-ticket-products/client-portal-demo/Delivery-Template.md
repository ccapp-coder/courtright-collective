## Internal notes, delete before sending

**Time budget: 5 to 7 hours across 7 days. This is the most expensive service in the library to deliver, so protect the scope.**

### Intake, before you build anything, 30 min

- [ ] What do clients email you about most?
- [ ] What documents move back and forth?
- [ ] What do clients ask for status on?
- [ ] What do you currently do by hand every single time?
- [ ] What tools are already in play? (QuickBooks, Calendly, Dropbox, their CRM)
- [ ] Who else has to approve a purchase like this?
- [ ] Brand assets: logo, colors, fonts

### Build, 4 to 5 hours

- [ ] Start from the Indie App Starter Kit scaffold. Do not start from zero, ever
- [ ] Their colors and logo in, first. It is what makes the demo land
- [ ] Three to five screens maximum. **Resist building more**
- [ ] Realistic fake data using their actual client types and their actual vocabulary. Generic placeholder data kills the illusion instantly
- [ ] Works on a phone. They will open it on a phone first, guaranteed
- [ ] A demo login that cannot break, and cannot be broken by clicking around
- [ ] Deploy to a subdomain. Set a calendar reminder to take it down at 30 days

### Write-up, 45 min

- [ ] Fill in this template
- [ ] Three specific click paths, tested in order
- [ ] Prices for all three tiers, decided before you send, never "we can discuss"

### Rules

- **Be relentlessly clear about what is faked.** Overselling a demo is how you end up with a client expecting a product in two weeks
- Their words on every screen. Their client types, their job names, their vocabulary
- **Five hours is the cap.** At $149 this is deliberately close to break-even. It is a sales tool, not a profit center, and it converts at a rate that makes that fine
- The honest assessment section is required. Sometimes the answer is a shared folder, and saying so is what makes the yes credible
- Reuse aggressively. After three of these you have a component library and the build drops to two hours
