## Sales blurb

**Your studio is currently held together by three notes apps and your memory.**

I know because mine was. Ideas in the phone notes, tasks in a text file, revenue in whatever the App Store dashboard told me last, and a launch checklist I rewrote from scratch every single release because I never wrote it down properly.

The Indie Studio Command Center is the fix. One Notion workspace that runs the whole thing. It is the actual system I use for Courtright Collective across multiple apps, client work, and whatever new thing I talked myself into last week.

**Eleven connected databases:**

- **Apps and Projects.** Every app, client job, and side thing, on one board. Including a Graveyard view, because killing things is a skill and you should be able to see the proof that you have it.
- **Tasks.** One list for everything, with an Effort column so that when you have twenty free minutes at 11pm you can filter to the twenty-minute jobs instead of reading your whole life.
- **Idea Vault.** Three honest questions, one score formula, no more 2am idea loops.
- **Launch Command.** With the full 40-point launch checklist. Two weeks out, one week out, launch day, one week after. Duplicate it into every release and stop reinventing it.
- **Revenue Log and Expenses.** Simple enough that you will actually keep it up. Including the recurring-expenses view that will pay for this template on its own.
- **Metrics.** One row per app per month. Fifteen minutes a month buys you a year of history.
- **Support and Feedback.** With a Count column, so you stop guessing what to build next and start reading it off a sorted list.
- **Content Calendar.** Never open a blank page again.
- **Ship Log.** One line per thing shipped, one line on what you learned. On the bad weeks, you open this and scroll. That is what it is for.
- **Weekly Review.** Twenty minutes every Friday, including the question most founders skip: what am I avoiding?
- **Studio Assets.** The page you will frantically search for at the worst possible moment.

Import it, spend twenty minutes turning the tables into real databases, pin it to your sidebar. Done.

**$49. Setup takes about 20 minutes. Then it just runs.**

> One rule I put right in the template: do not customize it for two weeks. Use it as-is, notice what actually annoys you, then change that one thing. Every Notion template on earth dies from people redesigning it instead of using it.
