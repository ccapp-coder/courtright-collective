## Sales blurb

**I have signed up for six CRMs. I have paid for three. I have used zero of them for longer than five weeks.**

Here's the thing. Real CRMs are built for sales teams. Lead scoring, territory assignment, forty fields per record, because a twelve person sales org needs all that and somebody's manager needs to see it.

You are one person. You do not have territories. You have about eleven things going on and a memory that is currently storing all of them in a way that wakes you up at 4am.

A one-person business needs to answer four questions. Who owes me an answer. Who do I owe an answer to. What is actually going to close this month. And who have I not talked to in too long.

Three sheets. All four questions. No monthly bill.

**What is in it:**

- **Pipeline.** Nine stages with a written rule for what has to be true before a deal moves forward, because pipelines lie in exactly one direction and it is always the hopeful one. Fixed probability by stage so your gut stops grading its own homework, plus a weighted pipeline number that is roughly honest about what is coming.
- **Contacts.** Warmth ratings, touch cadences by relationship type, and a Personal Notes column that costs five seconds and turns a vendor into somebody people actually like hearing from.
- **Activity Log.** Sixty seconds after every call. Saves you the day a client says "you told me four thousand" and you can open the exact row from six weeks ago.
- **Conditional formatting rules** so overdue follow-ups turn red on their own and any deal with no next action lights up bright red until you fix it.
- **Eight formulas,** including the one that tells you where your money actually comes from, which is almost never where you assumed.
- **The weekly rhythm.** Twenty minutes Monday, ten minutes Friday. That runs the entire business development side of a one-person company.
- **When to leave.** Four specific signs you have outgrown a spreadsheet, so you upgrade when you actually need to instead of when a sales page tells you to.

**$19. Ten minute setup. No subscription, ever.**

> A CRM does not sell anything. You do. What this fixes is the specific and expensive problem of good opportunities dying quietly because nobody wrote down what happens next.

## Notes and assumptions

- Priced at $19 to be an obvious yes against a $50 a month tool the buyer is currently guilty about not using.
- Delivered as CSV rather than a Sheets template link so buyers own the files and there is no sharing-permissions mess.
- The sample pipeline rows deliberately model Courtright Collective's own services (website rebuild, email triage automation, monthly reports) with placeholder company names. It demonstrates the sheet and quietly shows the buyer what this studio does, without being a pitch.
- One sample row shows a lead that arrived through the free QR menu tool, and another shows a lead that arrived after buying the Legal Intake Checklist. That is the product library's own funnel, modeled inside the product. Nice touch for anyone paying attention.
- Best upsell path: CRM Starter, then the free 15-minute audit, then Email Automation.
- Good bundle partner: Founder's Copy Bank (the email templates fill the follow-ups this sheet reminds you to send). Call it "The Follow-Up Kit" at $39.
