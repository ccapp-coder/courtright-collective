## The offer page

### You know something needs to change. You do not know which thing.

The website is old. The Google listing might be wrong. You have a Facebook page you have not touched since 2023 and an Instagram you post to when you remember. Somebody told you to do reels. Somebody else said email. Your cousin mentioned SEO.

None of that is a plan. It is a pile of advice, and it is why the whole thing has stayed on your list for two years.

**Get on a call with me for 45 minutes.** I look at everything beforehand, we talk it through, and then I write down what to do, in order, with who does it and how long each thing takes.

**How it works:**

1. **Before the call,** I look at your site on a phone, run the searches your customers run, check your Google profile, and look at whichever social account you actually use.
2. **On the call,** you talk first. Where do customers come from now, what do you wish happened more, what have you already tried and hated. Then I tell you what I found, starting with what is working, because there always is something.
3. **We build the plan out loud, together.** Every item gets tested with "could you actually do that?" If the answer is no, the item is wrong and we change it.
4. **Within 24 hours,** you get it in writing: this week, this month, this quarter, with who and how long on every single line.

**Also in the write-up: what to ignore.** Two or three things somebody has been pushing you toward that are not worth your time yet, each with what would have to be true before they become worth doing. That section is usually the most useful one in the document.

**$99. Forty-five minutes. A written plan you could hand to anybody, including yourself.**

> A promise about the call: I will talk less than half the time. If I talked more than you did, I sold instead of listened, and you paid for the wrong thing. And if it turns out you do not need me for any of it, I will say so and refund it.

## Notes and assumptions

- **Priced at $99.** Same as the Website Teardown on purpose, so the two read as siblings. The Teardown is for somebody who knows the problem is the website. This is for somebody who does not know what the problem is, which is a much larger group.
- **Total time is about 90 minutes:** 10 minutes of prep, a 45-minute call, and 30 to 40 minutes writing it up. That is a fair rate and it stays fair because the template makes the write-up fast.
- **The 24-hour write-up rule is not negotiable.** The value of a call like this decays fast, and so does the goodwill. A plan that arrives four days later gets skimmed and filed.
- **"Talk less than half the time" is the operating rule** and it is stated on the offer page, which makes it a promise rather than an aspiration. Small business owners have been talked at by enough agencies.
- **The "what to ignore" section is the trust engine,** exactly like the "what I would leave alone" section in the Website Teardown. Telling somebody not to do something you could charge for is the fastest way to be believed about everything else.
- The internal notes include a call script broken into five-minute blocks, so the call does not drift and does not run long.
- Cap these at a few a week. It is a high-touch product and it stops being good the moment it feels routine.
- Best upsell path: the plan itself lists the pieces and the prices, so the upsell is built into the deliverable. No separate pitch required.
